01 · Your story on one page

How to write a CV

A CV is a focused snapshot of your experience, skills and potential. Make the most relevant evidence easy to spot.

01

Keep it clear and complete

Add your contact details, then organise your profile, skills, education and relevant experience or projects. Put the strongest section first.

02

Show why you fit

Tailor your CV to the role. Use action-led examples to show relevant skills and results you can explain honestly.

03

Make it readable and safe

Use clear headings and a simple layout. Leave out sensitive details like your ID and banking information.

Achievement formula: Action + task or context + result. Example: “Organised a weekly peer study group for 12 classmates, helping the team complete two group projects on schedule.”

02 · A focused introduction

How to write a cover letter

A strong letter connects your experience to this specific opportunity. It should add context to your CV, not repeat every line.

  1. Start with the role.Name the position and organisation, and say why it interests you.
  2. Show your fit.Share one or two relevant examples, including school or volunteer projects.
  3. Close clearly.Thank the reader and sign off professionally.

Turn everyday experience into evidence

You do not need a formal job title to show useful skills. Use a real school project, volunteer role, personal project, community activity or responsibility. This helper only uses the details you enter here.

What can count?

  • A class assignment or group project
  • Helping a club, team, faith or community group
  • Learning a skill or building something independently
  • Taking responsibility for organising, supporting or solving a problem

Keep it truthful: use real details; never add a result, number or skill you cannot explain.

Nothing is uploaded or saved. Review the draft and make sure every statement is accurate and sounds like you.

Protect yourself while applying

  • Never pay to apply: Be cautious of requests for application, interview, uniform or “processing” fees.
  • Check the source: Look for the vacancy on the organisation’s official website and verify the sender’s email domain.
  • Pause at pressure: Urgent offers, guaranteed jobs and requests for banking details or one-time passwords are warning signs.
  • Share personal data carefully: Do not send your ID or financial documents to an unverified recruiter.
